Issue 026 The Accountability Gap

Nigeria Has Charged Four Civil Servants Over Its Fake Agency. The Only Fix So Far Asks for a Document the Fake Agency Had and the Real One Never Did.

The ICPC's amended charge adds four officers, none above assistant director. The payments reported from it are N210,000 and N300,000. Its own interim report describes offices that handed over a budget code, 314 posts and a place in the Appropriation Act without checking that the council existed in law. The police had charged the man 22 days before the President presented a budget with his council in it. Four months after the scandal broke, the one written reform is a sentence in a budget circular telling agencies to attach their Establishment Acts. The fake council had one.

By Obongodu Paul Unanam. Published Thursday 12 November 2026. Nine minutes. On public money.

On Monday Adeniyi Adeyemi was brought from Kuje to a courtroom in Maitama for a bail hearing and found that his case had grown. The ICPC had filed an amended charge on November 3. The 22 counts he denied last month are now 36, and four civil servants have been added to the sheet. Nobody took a plea, because the prosecution said one of the four was in hospital, and Justice Yusuf Halilu adjourned until November 18.

Readers who were here in July know the name. Adeyemi is the man who presented himself as Director General of a Presidential Foreign Intervention Promotion Council, a body which, in the ICPC's words, "was not created by any law, Executive Order or other valid government instrument", and which sits at line 18 under the Presidency in the 2026 Appropriation Act with N1.3 billion against it. The ICPC has called it Investment, as I did in Issue 008. The State House and the Act say Intervention. Nobody has settled the name of a thing that never existed.

The four are Aminu Abdullahi, who handled office allocation for the Secretary to the Government of the Federation; Ogaba Harry, a chief accountant; Florence Omameh, an assistant director in the Accountant General's inspectorate; and Patricia Akhigbe, an assistant director in the budget ministry. None has been arraigned and none has answered the charge. As reported, Abdullahi is alleged to have taken N210,000 to arrange the council's rooms at the Federal Secretariat, and Harry N300,000 to release two official vehicles to it. The ICPC's interim report, as Premium Times read it in August, mentions sums of up to N3.25 million.

I have no quarrel with the charge. If officers took money to open doors for him, they should answer for it. But set the sums beside what the council came away with. An administrative code from the Accountant General. An approved establishment of 314 posts from the Head of the Civil Service. Two domiciliary accounts at the Central Bank. A line in an Act of the National Assembly. An audience with the chairman of the EFCC, who told his visitors: "If somebody sets out to scam the system, the long arm of the law will catch up with such a person." Add up every payment to an official that has been reported from the charge and the report and you do not reach N5 million. Either the Nigerian state is the cheapest thing ever sold, or most of this was never bought. I think most of it was never bought.

Follow the paper. On November 7, 2024 a letter claiming to come from State House asked the Accountant General for an administrative code. It was signed in the name of an officer who, investigators found, does not exist. The office issued the code and, by the House committee's account, let Adeyemi collect the reply himself. At the Head of Service's office the council showed an appointment letter and an Establishment Act and left with its 314 posts. In August 2025 the council sent the Budget Office a personnel budget of N3.85 billion, the details on a flash drive. The Budget Office disregarded the figure and worked out its own, N803 million, from the Head of Service's approval. It added N500 million of overhead and capital the council had not asked for. One line in the Act, as the journalism centre CJID read it, is N182.5 million for "logistics for preparation of hosting World Investment Summit 2026".

Now listen to the offices. The Head of the Civil Service told the House committee that creating agencies "is not within the purview" of her office. When she asked to see the papers herself, she said, "I saw that the Establishment Act was not really an authentic Act." She also said this: "Everything was done in accordance with the practice in the office." The Budget Office said on July 24 that it "acted on documents issued through recognised Government channels". I believe both. Three weeks ago I quoted the Central Bank branch controller who paid out $6.23 million in 2023 on a letter the court has been told was forged: "I only acted on what is on the paper." It is the same sentence.

The parts of the ICPC's interim report that have reached the press agree with them more than the charge sheet suggests. The Head of Service's procedure did not adequately require a new institution to produce its establishment documents. There was no evidence the council had formally applied for its posts, and the file was reported missing when investigators asked for it. The Budget Office held only a scanned copy of the approval, and there was no evidence anyone there checked the instrument that created the council. "These weaknesses," the report says, "created a control gap that enabled the PFIPC to be included in 2026 budget."

The report named 13 officers for administrative action. Eight work in the Accountant General's office. Three are directors, and none of the three is charged. I am not saying the rest belong in the dock. The criminal law reaches the officer who takes money. It has nothing to say to the officer who follows a procedure that asks no questions, and it should not. That is why a trial cannot be the answer here. I have looked for any announcement of what became of the other nine and found none.

The Budget Office's defence deserves a hearing, because half of it is right. "An appropriation is authority in law to make provision for an expenditure. It is not a cheque. It is not a warrant." True. "Not one kobo was drawn." The ICPC says the same, and the Central Bank says the two accounts "have never been operated", because the council never sent in its signatories. Then comes the sentence I cannot accept: "The money never moved because the controls held."

Perhaps the gates further down would have held. Nobody tested them. The gate that decides who is in the budget at all stood open, and it stayed open long after the centre of government knew. The Chief of Staff asked the police and the DSS to investigate on October 17, 2025. On November 5 he wrote to the SGF's office denying Adeyemi and what the Presidency calls "his spurious agency". On November 27 the police filed an eight count charge. On December 19, 22 days later, the President presented a budget with the council in it, and it passed both chambers into law. By the Budget Office's own statement, questions about the council's legal status arose in June 2026, about seven months after that letter.

And cash is the wrong test. The charge says Adeyemi collected N20 million from a company's managing director in July 2025, as a "project facilitation loan" against a contract of N2.8 billion. The House committee says a businessman paid him about N400 million in five instalments between May and July of that year. If any of that is proved, his money was never going to come out of the treasury. It came from people who trusted the treasury's paper.

Not one kobo was drawn, and I believe it. But the state lent its name, one office after another, and the name is what was sold.

So what has been fixed? The Budget Office's call circular for 2027 personnel budgets, dated September 4, says "it has become compulsory for MDAs to submit budget proposals along with their respective Establishment Acts", and that failure "may lead to rejection". It is the only change of procedure I can find in writing. Adeyemi's council had an Establishment Act. It showed one to the Head of Service's office, and the House committee has since found that neither chamber passed it and that parts of another institution's Act appear to have been altered to make it. The genuine council whose name and offices were taken over, the Presidential Economic Advisory Council, was announced in a press statement by President Buhari's spokesman on September 16, 2019 and sworn in on October 9. The Buhari Media Organisation said in July that it was ad hoc and purely advisory, with no budget line. I have looked for an Act or a gazetted order behind it and found none.

The real council was announced in a press statement and never had an Act. The fake one had an Act. A rule that tells every agency to attach its Act asks for the paper the forger had already thought of.

Asking for a document stops nobody who can make one. What stops him is an official able to check the document against something he does not control. As far as I can find, Nigeria keeps no public register of federal bodies showing the Act or presidential instrument that created each one and where it was gazetted, so office after office had nowhere to look. In August the ICPC announced it had found another fake agency, run by another man, inside the SGF's own premises. The House asked for a register of sorts on July 8, the day before I did: the Budget Office should send, with every appropriation bill, a certified list of every body proposed for funding and the law that created it. Section 19 of the Fiscal Responsibility Act lists six documents that must accompany the annual budget. A list of who is being funded, and by what right they exist, is not one of them. One paragraph would make it the seventh.

The House gave its committee four weeks. That was 18 weeks ago. The committee published interim findings on September 2 and promised a final report. The ICPC's report of August 6 was interim too, and the measures it was told to recommend "to prevent the recurrence of similar abuses" have not been published in any detail. The Minister of State for Budget said on October 28 that "the intention is to present" the 2027 budget "before the end of the year". That leaves seven weeks for the certified list to exist.

On November 18 five people are due before Justice Halilu, and in time a court will say who forged what and who was paid. I keep going back to the Head of Service's sentence, because I think it is the most honest thing anyone has said about this. Everything was done in accordance with the practice in the office. When the 2027 bill arrives I will open the list of agencies under the Presidency and count how many I can find a law for. I do not know what the number will be. On this year's evidence, I am not sure the office that prints the list knows either.
